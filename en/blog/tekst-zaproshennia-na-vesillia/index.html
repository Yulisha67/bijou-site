What every invitation must say

Whatever the style, a guest should understand five things right away:

  • Who is inviting — your names (and the parents' names, if you like).
  • Who is invited — address guests by name: “Dear Olena and Andrii” feels warmer than “Dear guests”.
  • When — the date, the day of the week and the start time.
  • Where — the venue name and address. If the ceremony and the reception are in different places, list both.
  • Reply by — so you can confirm the headcount with the venue.

Everything else — dress code, gift wishes, transfers, the schedule — can go in a separate block, so the main text stays short.

Classic texts

Good for a formal wedding when you want something restrained and elegant.

Dear [guest names],

We invite you to share with us the day we become a family. We would be honoured to see you at our wedding on [date] at [time] at [venue].

With love, [Name] and [Name]

[Name] and [Name] request the pleasure of your company at their wedding ceremony on [date] at [time], [address].

The celebration continues at [venue].

Dear [guest names],

A special day is coming in our lives, and we want the people closest to us beside us. Please join us on [date]. The ceremony begins at [time] at [venue], and we would love to celebrate with you the evening after.

We are happy to announce that on [date] we are getting married! We would be grateful if you shared this day with us and witnessed the beginning of our story together.

[Name] and [Name]

Warm and heartfelt

For couples who want the invitation to feel personal, like a letter from people who love their guests.

Dear ones,

We have waited a long time for this day, and now it is almost here. We really want the people who know us best, who laugh with us and support us, to be there. So we are waiting for you on [date] at [time] at [venue].

This day would not be complete without you.

Once we simply happened to meet. Now we have decided never to part again.

Please join us for our wedding on [date]. It will be warm, sincere and a little bit emotional — we promise.

Our story began with [a coffee / one message / a shared trip]. The next chapter is on [date], and we very much want you to see it.

We are waiting for you at [time] at [venue].

Dear [guest names],

They say happiness is having your own people nearby. So on the happiest day of our lives, we want to see exactly you. [Date], [time], [venue].

Short and modern

Few words, a lot of meaning. These work well in an online invitation, where the details sit in separate blocks.

We're getting married!

[Date] · [Venue]

We'd love to have you with us.

Save the date: [date].

[Name] + [Name]. Details coming soon.

One day. Two people. And you among the most important guests.

[Date], [time], [venue].

We said yes to each other. Now it's your turn — say yes to our invitation.

[Date] · [Venue]

With a bit of humour

If your guests are friends who share your sense of humour.

We decided that together is more fun than apart. We're making it official on [date] and inviting you to witness this adventure.

Food, dancing and our happy faces — guaranteed.

After [number] years together, we've finally agreed who gets the bigger half of the bed. Let's celebrate this agreement on [date] at [time] at [venue].

Attention! On [date] two people voluntarily give up single life. Your presence is mandatory, and so is a good mood.

We're getting married, which means you have a legitimate excuse to wear your best outfit and dance until morning. See you on [date] at [venue].

From the parents

The traditional option, when the parents of the couple host the wedding.

[Bride's parents' names] and [groom's parents' names] are delighted to invite you to the wedding of their children, [Name] and [Name], on [date] at [time] at [venue].

Dear [guest names],

Our children, [Name] and [Name], are starting a family. We ask you to share this joy with us on [date] at [time]. We would be truly glad to see you among our guests.

With respect, the [surname] and [surname] families

For an online invitation

Online invitations are a good place for a short, warm text, while the date, the map, the schedule and the RSVP button sit in their own blocks. Guests open everything from their phones, through one link.

Dear [guest names]!

We are getting married and we very much want you beside us. Below you'll find everything about our day: the location on the map, the schedule and the dress code. Please reply by [date].

[Name] and [Name] invite you to their wedding! Scroll down for the date, the venue and the button to confirm your attendance.

Dress code, gifts, kids and RSVP lines

These are best kept apart from the main text, so it doesn't feel overloaded.

Dress code

We would be grateful if you could match the colours of our celebration: [colours]. Please avoid white — that's the bride's colour.

Dress code: black tie. Gentlemen in a tuxedo or dark suit, ladies in an evening gown.

Gifts

Your presence is the best gift. If you'd still like to treat us, we'll be grateful for a contribution to our new home.

Instead of flowers, which fade by the next day, we'd love a bottle of wine — we'll open it on our first anniversary.

Children

We love your children dearly, but we're planning this evening as a celebration for adults. Thank you for understanding.

RSVP

Please confirm your attendance by [date] — it helps us prepare everything well.

Let us know whether you'll be with us by [date]: just tap the button below and leave your reply.

What to avoid

  • A generic “Dear guests”. A personal greeting by name feels completely different.
  • Requirements about the gift amount. It's enough to gently mention that you'd appreciate a card.
  • Overly long poems. Four lines at most, and better your own than one copied from the internet.
  • All the details in one paragraph. Date, address, dress code and schedule are easier to read as separate blocks.
  • No reply date. Otherwise replies keep arriving until the last day.
